# Kuşluk — Paper Afterlife / A4–A3 Fold and Cut Atlas (research v0.1)

**Date:** 2026-10-16 · **Issue:** #25 · **Status:** researched and dimensionally validated; no physical material trials yet.

## Concept

An already duplex-printed personal newspaper can become a folded, sculptural, or assembled artifact rather than always being thrown out. **Recycling remains the default and craft is strictly optional.** The first opportunity is to test no-cut rectangular forms before designing square origami that sacrifices nearly one-third of the area.

Three artistic directions to retain as inspiration, **not authorized collaborators**:
1. **Preserve words — Cecilia Levy**: layered, visible-text papier-mâché: https://www.cecilialevy.com/
2. **Preserve graphic surface — Will Kurtz**: sculptural newspapers with printed surface: https://www.willkurtz.com/work
3. **Reconstitute fibers — Jacqueline de la Fuente / de la Jardin**: pulp and paper-clay vessels: https://www.delajardin.com/

Artist attribution, image reproduction, licensing and potential interviews/commissions are separate questions; no outreach initiated.

## A4 and A3 precise geometry (nominal sheet dimensions)

| Operation | A4 (210 × 297 mm) | A3 (297 × 420 mm) | Unallocated sheet area | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4-panel rectangular concertina | fold lines 74.25, 148.5, 222.75 mm across long dimension | fold lines 105, 210, 315 mm | **0%** | physical fold test |
| Two-cut pop-up step | fold at 105 mm across width; two cuts from folded spine, reverse-fold inner tab | fold at 148.5 mm; geometrically scale cuts | **0%** (slits, no removed fiber) | validate geometry physically |
| Two equal square sheets | **2 × 148.5 mm** square + **61.5 × 297 mm** strip | **2 × 210 mm** square + **87 × 420 mm** strip | **29.2857% / 29.2929%** offcut strip, potentially reusable | test offcut alternatives |
| Five mixed-scale square sheets | **1 × 210, 2 × 87, 2 × 36 mm**, remaining **36 × 15 mm** | **1 × 297, 2 × 123, 2 × 51 mm**, remaining **51 × 21 mm** | **0.8658% / 0.8586%** | test fine cuts and actual usefulness |

The math is area allocation, **not measured ecological footprint**. Cutting kerf, sheet tolerances, print margins, paper grain, and grip/crease allowance were not modeled. Treat the >99% coverage as a design possibility, not a validated zero-waste claim. Square sizes may be too small for the desired physical artifacts.

For very few steps, rectangular no-cut models win. To make truly *two equal squares*, the residual strip is substantial. If **unequal** square sizes are acceptable, multiple nested modules can dramatically reduce unused area. This is the best new geometry finding.

Geometry sources: [DIN A-format dimensions](https://www.din.de/de/din-und-seine-partner/presse/mitteilungen/herzlichen-glueckwunsch-a4--878664) and [ISO 216:2007](https://committee.iso.org/cms/live/live/en/sites/isoorg/contents/data/standard/03/66/36631.html?browse=ics).

## Print-experience constraints

- Reused newspaper sheets have print on *both* faces. Avoid models that presume blank verso or page imposition.
- Prefer instructions on screen or a re-usable jig: printing a new guide sheet to reuse an old one defeats the minimum-material premise.
- Check toner cracking, ink show-through, fold fatigue and stiffness by stock, fiber direction, and printer type.
- Avoid disclosing a reader's private content or subscription-derived text when posting craft photos publicly.
- Cut-and-fold pop-up may require scissors or craft knife; provide safer no-tool alternatives.
- Keep precise print geometry at 100% size; home-printing nonprintable areas can hide marks, so crease measurement from physical sheet edges is the fallback.
- Papier-mâché and paper clay may introduce glues/coatings that impair subsequent recycling; decorative paper objects are not inherently food-safe.

## Experimental priorities

1. **No-cut**: fold already-duplex-printed A4 and A3 into concertina, pocket card, and rectangle box. Record stability and legibility.
2. **No-offcut kirigami**: test a two-slit pop-up card; record mechanical success, cut/fold safety, and legibility.
3. **Multiscale cuts**: cut 1 large + 2 medium + 2 small squares; use them in a related origami family, and explore tiny remaining strips as labels/tabs.
4. Compare on at least two uncoated recycled paper stocks, ideally both inkjet and toner printing.
5. Evaluate time, tools, tear/fold performance, tactile value, new materials added, actual offcuts, storage value and recycling options. Photograph genuine outcomes **only after making them**.

## Completion gate for #25

- Credited reference board and image permissions reviewed.
- ≥10 original or legally reusable template candidates cataloged with precise license / designer attribution.
- Source-controlled SVG/DXF/PDF patterns with dimensional and print-safe tests.

**Design north star:** A newspaper can be temporary, but its afterlife should be chosen by the reader—not imposed by the product.
